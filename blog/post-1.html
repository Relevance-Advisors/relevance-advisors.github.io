Why 36 Hours? Our Cold-Ferment Sourdough Process

By Marguerite Sable · July 2, 2026

Every loaf that leaves Sable & Rye starts the same way: a levain built the night before, mixed into dough the next morning, shaped by early afternoon, and then — instead of going straight into the oven — moved into our walk-in cooler for a 36-hour cold ferment.

That long, slow rise at low temperature does two things a same-day bake can't. First, it gives the wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria in our starter time to fully break down the starches and gluten, which is a big part of why people who say wheat "doesn't agree" with them often do fine with our bread. Second, it builds flavor — the tang in a real sourdough comes almost entirely from fermentation time, not from vinegar or citric acid the way some commercial "sourdough" breads fake it.

We taste-test every batch before it goes in the wood-fired oven each morning, adjusting proof time by feel rather than the clock — cold ferments run differently depending on humidity and how active the starter was that week.
